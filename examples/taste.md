# Taste lens: worked example

Two fresh agent sessions reviewed the same draft with the same prompt: "Review this partner email before I send it." Both had my global instructions loaded. The only difference is that the second one read [`lenses/taste.md`](../lenses/taste.md) first. Both ran on the same model, on 2026-10-11.

The email below is a fictional sample written for this test. No real partner, number or company is in it.

## The draft

> **Subject: Exciting updates to our partner program!**
>
> Hi team,
>
> We hope this finds you well! As part of our ongoing commitment to delivering the best possible experience for our partners, we're thrilled to share some exciting changes to how our program works.
>
> Over the past year, we've been listening closely to your feedback and investing heavily in our platform. We've made significant improvements to offer quality, targeting, and reporting, and we're confident these changes will help drive even stronger results for everyone.
>
> Starting next month, we'll be moving to a new payout structure. Revenue share will be adjusted to better reflect the value delivered across the network, and payments will move from net-30 to net-45 to align with our advertiser billing cycles.
>
> We believe this positions us well for continued growth together, and we're excited about what's ahead. Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions!
>
> Best,
> The Partnerships Team

## Without the lens (excerpts)

> Do not send as written. The email announces a revenue share cut and a slower payment schedule behind promotional language. Partners will read it as spin.
>
> 1. Subject is misleading for a payout reduction…
> 2. The news is buried in paragraph 3…
> 3. "Revenue share will be adjusted" doesn't say direction or amount…
>
> Do partner agreements allow changing rev share and payment terms by email? Check notice periods and consent or amendment requirements…
>
> Suggested skeleton: Subject "Payout changes effective [date]". Para 1: what changes… Para 2: why… Para 3: named contact, offer to talk.

A good review: seven numbered problems, a legal check and a skeleton for the rewrite.

## With the lens (excerpts)

> **Job:** Tell partners their payout terms are getting worse without losing their trust.
>
> **Verdict: Not yet.**
>
> **Why:** This is a change notice that hides the change. The two numbers partners care about, the new rev share and the exact start date, are missing. A partner who reads "adjusted to better reflect the value delivered" will read it as a cut and trust you less for the euphemism. They also can't forward it as is: their finance team would first have to ask what the new share is.
>
> - Cut test: delete paragraphs 1 and 2 and nothing is lost.
> - Money is not findable: no new rev share percentage, no effective date ("next month" is not a date)…
>
> **Fixes:**
>
> Subject: Payout changes effective [date]: rev share and payment terms
>
> Hi [first name],
>
> Two changes to your payout take effect on [date]:
>
> - Revenue share moves from [X]% to [Y]%.
> - Payment terms move from net-30 to net-45. Earnings from [date] onward are paid on the new schedule. Anything earned before then is paid on net-30.
>
> For a partner earning [example monthly amount], that works out to [example impact]. [One verified sentence on what improved, with the number. Cut this line if there's no verified figure.]
>
> If you want to talk it through before [date], reply to me directly or book time here: [link]. I'll go through your account's numbers on the call.
>
> [Name], [Title], [Company]

## What changed

- **A verdict replaced a list.** "Not yet," with one paragraph of why, tells the sender what to do. Seven equal-weight problems don't.
- **The why is tied to the email's job.** Both reviews spotted the euphemism. Only the lens review explained the cost: a partner trusts you less, and their finance team bounces it back.
- **The fix arrived as finished text** with placeholders, not a skeleton to fill in. It's ready to send once the numbers go in.
- **The money got specific.** It split old and new earnings on the net-45 switch and added a worked dollar example.

## What the lens missed

The review without the lens asked whether the partner contracts allow changing terms by email, with notice periods and amendment clauses. The lens review didn't. That's a real gap. It belongs in the commercial lens, not taste, which is why taste runs last and isn't the only check.
