# Commercial lens: worked example

Two fresh agent sessions got the same renewal email and the same prompt: "What position should I take, and how should I respond?" Both had my global instructions loaded. The only difference is that the second read [`lenses/commercial.md`](../lenses/commercial.md) first. Same model, 2026-10-13.

The email is a fictional sample written for this test.

## The email

> Ahead of renewal, we need a few changes:
> 1. Revenue share moves from 50/50 to 70/30 in our favor.
> 2. A guaranteed minimum of $40,000 a month, paid regardless of performance.
> 3. Exclusivity: you don't work with any other food delivery app.
>
> Two competitors have pitched us better terms, so we need an answer by Friday.

## Without the lens (excerpts)

> Don't accept as written, and don't walk away. Counter on the economics, and treat exclusivity as the main thing you sell.
>
> **Guarantee:** reframe it as an advance recoupable against future earnings. Size it well below $40k.
>
> **Walk-away line:** a guarantee plus a worse split plus exclusivity together. Any one of the three can be negotiated. All three cannot.
>
> Reply by Friday to acknowledge and propose a call. A deadline set by the other side isn't yours.

A sound review. It reads the pressure correctly and proposes a sensible package.

## With the lens (excerpts)

> **What the ask costs you**
> - **Split:** your share falls from 50% to 30%, a 40% cut per dollar. Their volume has to grow about 67% just to leave your margin where it is today. Nothing in the email says why it would.
> - **Guarantee:** $480K a year, owed regardless of performance. At a 70% partner share it stops being a loss only if monthly gross is above about $57K.
> - **Exclusivity:** the one term they've offered nothing for.
>
> | They get | You ask for |
> |---|---|
> | A tiered split: 50/50 to a volume threshold, better for them above it | A 12-month term and a volume commitment |
> | A smaller guarantee, recoupable against rev share, ramped | A per-unit minimum and a clear exit if either side misses |
> | Narrow exclusivity for 12 months | A premium for it, plus guaranteed placements |
>
> **Needs sign-off:** anything under your share floor needs a named approver. Exclusivity and any guarantee go to whoever owns revenue risk. Get this partner's share of your revenue first; exclusivity makes concentration worse.

## What changed

- **The cost became numbers.** "A 40% cut" and "volume must grow 67% to break even" turn a feeling of a bad deal into a threshold someone can check.
- **The guarantee got a break-even.** About $57K a month in gross is the line below which you pay out of pocket. The baseline said "size it well below $40k" without saying why.
- **It names who decides.** Terms below the floor, exclusivity and guarantees go to a named approver instead of being settled on the call.

## What the lens missed

The baseline's advice not to answer the substance by Friday is sharper than the lens version's "reply before Friday with one counter." The lens says to know your walk-away before the call, but said nothing about refusing the other side's deadline. That rule was added to the lens after this test.
